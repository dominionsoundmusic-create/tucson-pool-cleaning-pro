What a Weekly Visit Looks Like

Every visit follows the same routine, in the same order, so nothing gets skipped. The technician skims the surface, empties the skimmer basket and the pump basket, brushes the walls, steps, and waterline tile, and vacuums the floor. Then he tests the water — chlorine, pH, alkalinity, cyanuric acid, and calcium hardness — and adjusts whatever needs adjusting. He checks the filter pressure gauge, inspects the pump and any other visible equipment, and logs what he found and what he dosed. You get a clean pool and a record of what went into it.

The whole visit takes 20 to 40 minutes depending on the pool's size and condition. If something is wrong — a noisy pump bearing, a filter running high, a crack in the skimmer throat — you hear about it the same day, not after it has turned into a bigger problem.

Why Weekly Matters in Tucson

A pool in Portland or Chicago can go two weeks between cleanings and stay reasonable. A pool in Tucson cannot, and the reason is the climate working against you on three fronts at once.

UV and chlorine

Tucson averages over 300 days of direct sunlight a year. Ultraviolet light breaks down free chlorine — the chlorine that is actually killing bacteria — faster than almost any other factor. Without enough cyanuric acid to shield it, chlorine dosed on Monday can be gone by Wednesday. Weekly testing catches the drop before the pool turns.

Dust and debris

Desert dust is constant, and monsoon storms from mid-June through the end of September make it worse. A single haboob can deposit enough fine particulate to cloud a pool overnight and load the filter in one pass. Weekly brushing and vacuuming keep that dust off the plaster before it stains, and weekly basket emptying keeps the skimmer flowing instead of choking.

Hard water and calcium

Tucson tap water runs around 220 parts per million calcium hardness. As water evaporates — and it evaporates fast here — the calcium left behind concentrates. Without regular chemistry adjustments the saturation index drifts toward scaling, and calcium starts depositing on the tile line, inside the heater, and across the salt cell plates. Weekly testing is how you stay ahead of it instead of paying for tile cleaning and cell replacement more often than you should.

Monsoon Season

Mid-June to the end of September is a different season for pool maintenance. Storms hit fast, dump dust and organic debris, and raise humidity that feeds algae. Water temperature climbs past 90 degrees, and chlorine demand doubles.

During monsoon season a good pool service increases chemical dosing and spends more time on debris removal. If a storm hits between your regular visits and you wake up to green water or a pool full of leaves, ask whether an extra clean-up visit is included in your plan. On most service plans that extra visit is included — you should not have to pay twice because the weather was bad.

The only exception is a large one-time treatment like a full algae cleanout or a CYA drain-and-refill, which is quoted separately before any work.

Salt Water Pools

Salt pools still need weekly service. The salt cell generates chlorine, but it does not brush the walls, skim the surface, balance pH, or manage calcium. A good pool service inspects the cell each visit for scale buildup and cleans it when output drops — a scaled cell works harder, wears out faster, and costs over a thousand dollars to replace. If your salt pool needs a partial drain to lower calcium or CYA, remember that Pima County requires salt water to go to the sanitary sewer, not to landscape or a wash.

One-Time Deep Cleaning

If your pool has been sitting — a rental property between tenants, a house you just bought, a pool that was winterized and never reopened — a good pool service does one-time deep cleans to bring it back. That includes a full debris removal, a filter teardown and cleaning, a chemistry overhaul, brushing and vacuuming until the water clears, and a written report of anything that needs repair. You can stay on weekly service after or not — the deep clean stands on its own.

Pool Cleaning Questions

What is included in weekly pool cleaning?

Every visit includes skimming the surface, brushing walls and the tile line, vacuuming the floor, emptying skimmer and pump baskets, testing and adjusting water chemistry (chlorine, pH, alkalinity, cyanuric acid, calcium hardness), checking filter pressure, and inspecting visible equipment. Chemicals are included in the monthly price.

How often should a Tucson pool be cleaned?

Weekly is the standard for Tucson. Desert dust, UV that burns through chlorine, and hard water that shifts chemistry all happen faster here than in cooler climates. Biweekly can work for a lightly used pool outside of monsoon season, but weekly is more reliable and catches problems before they compound.

Are chemicals included or extra?

It depends on the company. Many pool services include routine chemicals such as chlorine, acid and stabilizer in the monthly price, while others bill them separately. Ask on the call and get the answer in writing. The only exception is a large one-time treatment like an algae cleanout or a full CYA drain-and-refill, which is quoted separately before any work.

What happens during monsoon season?

Monsoon storms dump dust, leaves, and debris into the pool and can turn water green within 48 hours. During monsoon season — mid-June through the end of September — a good pool service adjusts chemical dosing upward and spends more time on cleanup visits. If a storm hits between visits and you wake up to a green pool, ask whether an extra clean-up visit is included in your plan.

Do you service salt water pools?

Yes. Salt pools still need weekly brushing, chemistry testing, and equipment checks. A good service also inspects the salt cell for calcium scale and cleans it when output drops. The main difference with a salt pool in Pima County is draining: salt water must go to the sanitary sewer, not to landscape or a wash.
