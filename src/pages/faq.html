How often does pool service come in Tucson?

Once a week is the standard for most home pools, and it suits Tucson for most of the year. Every other week is the usual cheaper option. How often pool service comes to your yard is something you and the pool company agree on, so it helps to know what pushes the number up or down.

National cost guides from Angi describe weekly visits as the most common choice for pools that get used, with biweekly service as a lighter option for pools that see moderate use. A typical weekly visit covers skimming, brushing, emptying baskets, a water test and chemical adjustments, with filter care on a longer cycle. The exact list differs from company to company, so read it before you agree to a plan.

Tucson adds a few reasons to lean toward weekly. The National Weather Service in Tucson lists an average July high of about 100 degrees and around 68 days a year at 100 or above. Warm water and strong sun use up chlorine quickly. The monsoon, June 15 to September 30, brings wind, dust and leaf litter that land straight in the water. The Arizona Department of Water Resources puts open water evaporation here at about six feet a year, so the water level needs watching too.

No price on this page comes from the line. Published guides give a rough idea: HomeGuide puts national monthly maintenance at about $80 to $150 for weekly or every other week service. The pool company, directly, under whatever terms it gives you. The line never takes payment and never bills callers.

Pool care in Tucson

Why does my pool lose so much water?

Mostly evaporation. State water officials at the Arizona Department of Water Resources put the loss from open water in Tucson at about six feet a year, most of it in summer, and that a cover saves 90 to 95 percent of that loss. If the level drops faster than that, a leak is possible. The pool leak repair page explains the bucket test, a simple way to tell the two apart.

What is the white crust along my tile?

Usually calcium scale from hard water. Tucson Water says hardness rises as the share of Colorado River water from the CAP canal goes up in the blend, and evaporation leaves those minerals behind at the waterline. See pool tile cleaning for how removal methods compare.

What should I check after a monsoon storm?

Once the storm has passed and there is no lightning, empty the skimmer and pump baskets, pick up branches around the equipment, look at the water level and test the water. Heavy dust and leaves can clog a filter quickly. If the water turns cloudy or green, the green pool guide covers safe first steps.

Do I ever need to drain my pool?

Sometimes, when dissolved minerals build too high, but it is a big decision with local rules attached. The City of Tucson prefers that pool water go into the sanitary sewer through your own property's sewer cleanout, and filter backwash may stay on your lot only if it soaks in within 24 hours and does not reach neighbors, the street or an alley. Read draining a pool in Tucson before you start.

Repairs and licensing

Which pool work needs a licensed contractor in Arizona?

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors (ROC) issues an R-6 license, Residential Swimming Pool Service and Repair, for servicing and minor repairs on home pools. It does not cover gas lines, electrical work beyond the first disconnect, plumbing connections to the drinking water system, or a full replacement of a plaster or pebble interior. Building and major repair work falls under pool contractor licenses such as B-5. The ROC does not spell out whether cleaning alone needs a license, so ask the company which license it holds.

How do I check a pool company's license?

Ask for its ROC license number and type it into the ROC's online license lookup at roc.az.gov. The record shows whether the license is active, its classification, recent complaints and bond details. Check before work starts, since a license status can change. Checking is up to you; the line does not do it.

Does the ROC show whether a company is insured?

No. According to the ROC, the contractor search does not show insurance, and apart from workers' compensation the agency does not require contractors to carry it. Ask the company for proof of liability insurance yourself.

My pump stopped. What can I safely check?

Look at the timer, the baskets, the filter pressure gauge and whether a GFCI outlet or breaker has tripped. You can reset a tripped one a single time. Should it trip a second time, keep it off and get a professional. The pump troubleshooting guide lists the safe checks in order, and swimming pool repair covers what a repair visit involves.

Pool safety

Can I mix pool chemicals to save time?

Never. The CDC warns that mixing pool chemicals with each other can create toxic gases, and that chlorine products must never be mixed with acid. Keep each product in its original labeled container, stored dry, locked and out of children's reach, out of direct sun and below 95 degrees, and follow the label every time. Leave acid work to a professional.

I smell gas near the pool heater. What do I do?

Move away from the heater at once and bring everyone else with you. Do not touch the heater, light switches or anything that could make a spark. Once you are well clear, dial 911, then Southwest Gas on its round the clock leak line, 877-860-6020.

Is a green pool dangerous?

Yes, for two reasons. You cannot see the bottom, so a child or pet in trouble can be hidden from view: keep children away from it, and do not leave any child unwatched near a pool. A green pool can also breed mosquitoes, and Pima County asks residents to report neglected green pools to its mosquito line at 520-724-7908. The green pool cleanup page covers what a recovery involves. Separately, Arizona law and local codes set fencing and self-latching gate rules for many home pools, so keep gates closed and latched.
