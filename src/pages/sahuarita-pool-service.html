Sahuarita: a ranch name, pecan groves and fast growth

Sahuarita became an incorporated town on September 20, 1994. The Town says it had about 1,800 residents then; the 2020 Census counted 34,134, up from 25,259 in 2010. The name traces to the Sahuarito Ranch, which operated here from 1879 to 1886, and is said to mean "little saguaro." The Santa Cruz River runs through the area, and the Town describes significant acreage of pecan groves running through it.

Two big communities shape a lot of the housing. Rancho Sahuarita is a master-planned community that a Town economic plan described as planned for 11,680 homes. Quail Creek is an age-restricted community listed in the same plan at about 5,000 homes, with more units still moving through the Town's planning process. Census figures put roughly one resident in five at 65 or older, and about 81% of homes are owner occupied.

Seven water providers, one groundwater basin

Why it matters for a pool: every inch the sun takes out of the pool leaves its dissolved minerals behind, and the next top-up adds more. The Arizona Department of Water Resources figures open water in the Tucson region loses around six feet a year. Calcium hardness, a measure of how much calcium is dissolved in the pool, builds up until it shows as a white crust at the tile line or a rough feel on the plaster. Testing it a few times a year tells you whether scale is on its way, and pool tile cleaning explains what can be done once the crust appears.

Emptying a pool the way the Town asks

Sahuarita is an incorporated town, and its wastewater FAQ spells out how pool water should leave a property. When high calcium or dissolved solids mean the water has to be changed, these are the Town's points.

Your own cleanout

Send the water through the access cap of the private sewer cleanout on your property. That is the discharge point the Town names.

Keep the flow in check

Throttle the pump so the private sewer line, called the lateral, does not back up and spill pool water onto your yard or the house next door.

Stay out of the street

Never open a public manhole or a cleanout in the street. The Town warns that it disrupts traffic and puts people walking by, children and neighbors at risk.

TEP or Trico, by address

Both electric utilities serve parts of Sahuarita. Trico Electric Cooperative has a Sahuarita board district, and Tucson Electric Power has a transmission project, the Santa Rita Connection, serving Sahuarita and Green Valley. Your bill tells you which one runs your pump. On TEP's time of use plans, summer weekday afternoons from 3 to 7 p.m. are on-peak, so a pump timer set for the morning keeps the long summer runs out of that window. Check your own plan before changing the schedule.

Monsoon season on the south side

The National Weather Service in Tucson counts June 15 through September 30 as monsoon season. Storm winds strip leaves and dust off the desert and drop them in the water, and big rains can raise the water level and thin out the chlorine. A quick look at the skimmer, the pump basket and a fresh water test after each storm saves trouble later.
