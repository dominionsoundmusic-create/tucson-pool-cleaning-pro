Why Tucson pools need steady attention

A pool in the Sonoran Desert works harder than a pool almost anywhere else in the country. Four local conditions explain why weekly care is the habit for so many Tucson owners.

Heat and sun

National Weather Service normals for Tucson put the average July high at about 100 degrees, with 68 days a year reaching 100 or more. Warm water grows algae faster, and strong sunlight breaks chlorine down. That is why most outdoor pools here carry cyanuric acid, a stabilizer that shields chlorine from the sun. The CDC advises keeping free chlorine at 2 parts per million or more when stabilizer is in the water.

Monsoon storms and dust

Officially, the Tucson monsoon season runs from June 15 to the end of September, according to the weather service. Thunderstorm outflow can push walls of dust across the desert, and the storms that follow drop leaves, twigs and rain into the water. Filters load up, chlorine gets used up, and a pool that looked fine on Friday can be cloudy by Monday. If yours has already gone green, start with what to do when the pool is green.

Hard fill water

Tucson Water takes its share of Colorado River water, which arrives by canal through the Central Arizona Project (CAP), and sinks it into the aquifer in Avra Valley, where it blends with groundwater before it is pumped to homes. The utility says the water gets harder as the share of CAP water rises, and that hard water is safe to drink. In a pool, those minerals are what form the white crust along the tile and the scale inside heaters and salt cells. Tucson Water suggests testing pool water hardness with a test kit. Pool tile cleaning covers the crust once it builds up.

Evaporation

The Arizona Department of Water Resources puts open-water evaporation in Tucson at about six feet a year, most of it in summer. Every inch that leaves as vapor leaves its minerals behind, and the top-up water brings in more. Over the years that is why many pools eventually need part of their water replaced. According to ADWR, covering the pool keeps 90 to 95 percent of that water from escaping. The guide to draining or recycling pool water in Tucson explains the local rules on where that water may go.

Where the line takes calls

The line covers the City of Tucson and the communities around it in Pima County. Five of them have their own page about the local water, power and pool rules.

Tucson

Midtown, the east and west sides, the northwest and the southeast neighborhoods all call the same number. Inside city limits, Tucson Water and City of Tucson rules on draining and backwash apply.

Check a company before work starts

The company you are connected with is independent, so vetting it is up to you, and it only takes a few minutes. For weekly care, ask what each visit includes and who buys the chemicals. For any repair, get the quote on paper, plus the company's Arizona Registrar of Contractors (ROC) license number to search online. The ROC does not require pool contractors to carry liability insurance, so ask to see proof of it. The how it works page has a short checklist.
