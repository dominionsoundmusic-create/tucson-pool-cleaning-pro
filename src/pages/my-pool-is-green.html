My pool is green, what do I do? If that is the question in your head right now, start by slowing down. A green pool is fixable, and very few of the steps that matter in the first hour involve chemicals at all. What matters first is keeping people safe, then finding out whether the pump and filter are doing their job, then learning what the water test says.

This guide is written for Tucson homeowners. It covers the safe checks you can make, why pools here go green so quickly, the mistakes that make things worse, and the point where it is time to hand the job to a pool company. Tucson Pool Cleaning Pro does no pool work. It is a free referral line, and a call is handed to a Tucson-area pool business, locally owned and independent of the line, that inspects the pool and prices any work itself.

My pool is green: the first four things to do

1. Keep everyone away from the water

When the water is green you cannot see the floor. A small child who slips in would be out of sight almost at once. The CDC reports that drowning kills more children aged 1 through 4 than anything else, with home and other swimming pools the setting for most of them. Shut the pool gate, check that it latches, keep children and pets indoors and tell any guests to stay off the deck.

2. Check that water is moving

Look at the return jets: is water pushing out? Listen at the pump. If it is silent, check the timer or automation schedule, and check whether a GFCI outlet or breaker has tripped. You may reset it one time. A second trip suggests an electrical fault, so leave the power off and get help. If the pump hums but does not run, turn it off; the guide pool pump stopped working explains what that can mean.

3. Clear the baskets and read the gauge

With the pump off, tip out the skimmer and pump baskets. Then turn the pump back on and look at the pressure gauge on top of the filter. A reading well above where it normally sits after a cleaning usually means the filter is clogged and water is struggling to get through. Write the number down for the pool company.

4. Test the water

Use a home test kit or fresh strips and note pH, free chlorine and, if your kit shows it, stabilizer (cyanuric acid, which keeps sunlight from breaking chlorine down so fast). Very low chlorine is the usual finding. The numbers help whoever treats the pool, even if the reading looks confusing in cloudy water.

Why Tucson pools turn green so quickly

Algae spores reach every pool on the wind. They only take over when the chlorine runs out, and in Tucson that can happen fast. The National Weather Service puts Tucson's normal July high at about 100 degrees, with 68 days a year at 100 or above on average. Hot water under a strong sun uses chlorine up fast, and algae grows quickly once nothing is holding it back.

The pump stopped

A tripped breaker, a lost timer setting or a failed motor means no filtering and no chlorine spread around the pool. In summer, a few days is enough.

Chlorine burned off

The sun breaks chlorine down. Stabilizer slows that, which is why most outdoor pools here use it, but it cannot stop it, and an empty tablet feeder is easy to miss.

Stabilizer too low or too high

Too little and the sun strips chlorine within hours. Too much, from years of stabilized tablets, and the CDC notes chlorine works more slowly, so algae gets ahead.

A monsoon storm

The monsoon runs from June 15 to September 30. Rain, leaves and dust from a storm's outflow winds land in the water and use up sanitizer overnight.

Debris and phosphates

Leaves, pollen, bird droppings and some fertilizers add nutrients that feed algae. A pool with a lot of trees around it needs more attention.

Nobody was home

A summer away, a vacant rental or a missed service visit gives algae the weeks it needs to turn a pool dark.

What not to do with a green pool

Most of the damage to green pools comes from well-meaning shortcuts. These are the ones to avoid.

Do not combine pool chemicals

The CDC warns that pool chemicals should never be mixed with each other or with anything else, and that chlorine products combined with acid can release toxic gas. Never pour two products into the same bucket, and never use one container for different products.

Do not dump in whatever is in the shed

Pouring several products in at once, guessing at amounts, can push pH far out of range, cloud the water further, or raise stabilizer that then takes a drain to bring down. If you add anything, use one product, follow its label exactly and keep a note of what went in.

Do not drain it on a hot afternoon

Draining is not a quick fix and it has rules. The City of Tucson and Pima County want pool water sent to your own sewer cleanout where possible, after at least 72 hours without new chlorine and with pH between 7 and 8. A pool company can tell you whether draining is needed at all; read draining a pool in Tucson before anyone opens a valve.

Do not swim or let the kids in

Even a pale green pool can hide a child at the bottom of the deep end. Wait until the water is clear and tested.

Storing what you already have

While the pool waits for help, make sure the chemicals around it are safe. The CDC advises keeping pool chemicals in their original labeled containers, dry, locked away from children and pets, out of direct sunlight and below 95 degrees, with incompatible products stored apart. In a Tucson garage or side yard in summer, that last part takes some thought: a shaded, ventilated cabinet is better than an open shelf in the sun.

When to call a pool company

Call when any of these is true: you cannot see the floor, the pump will not run or keeps tripping the breaker, the filter pressure is high and stays high, you do not know what has been added to the water, or the house has been empty and the pool has been dark for a while. Call too if a neighbor's dark pool worries you; Pima County asks residents to report mosquito breeding problems, including green pools, to 520-724-7908.

When you speak with the company, give it what you found: the filter pressure, the test numbers, whether the pump runs, how long the pool has been green and anything that has been added. Ask what it thinks caused the problem and how many visits it expects. If repairs come up, request the company's Arizona Registrar of Contractors license number and enter it in the Registrar's online license lookup (opens in a new tab). Once the pool is clear, a regular routine keeps it that way; the Tucson pool maintenance schedule lays one out, and pool cleaning and weekly maintenance explains what a service visit covers.
