A phone line, not a pool company

Tucson Pool Cleaning Pro is a d/b/a of Dominion Digital Group. It is a free phone number plus this website, with a short request form for people who would rather type, and that is the whole operation.

There is no shop, no fleet and no crew behind the name. Nobody from the line will come to your house, test your water, look at your pump or hand you a price. What the line does is answer the phone at any hour, find out in a sentence or two what the pool is doing, and hand the call to a separate, locally owned pool business serving the Tucson area.

That company is the one that visits, figures out what is wrong, quotes the job and does it. It bills you itself. The line has no seat at that table and does not step in to set prices, dates or terms.

Spelling this out may seem like a lot of fuss over a phone number. It matters because plenty of pool websites read as if they belong to a company with trucks on the road. This one does not, and you should know who you are dealing with before you dial.

Why one number for every kind of pool problem

Pool trouble in the Tucson area comes in a lot of shapes, and it often shows up at a bad time. The line exists so you do not have to work out who to call first.

Problems do not keep office hours

A pump that quits on a 105 degree Saturday or a yard full of debris after a late monsoon storm cannot wait for Monday. The assistant picks up at every hour, on every day of the week, and if the company cannot take the call right then, it receives your details and can return the call.

One call covers most pool needs

Weekly cleaning, a green pool, calcium on the tile, a leak, a dead light, a noisy pump: you do not need to sort the job into a category first. Describe what you see and the call goes on from there.

A local company hears it first

The transfer goes to an independent, locally owned Tucson-area pool company, not a national call center. It gets a short summary of your problem before it picks up, so you are not starting from zero.

Help checking who you hire

Arizona's Registrar of Contractors keeps a public record of contractor licenses and complaints. This site walks you through searching that record, so the decision to hire stays yours and rests on facts you checked.

How the line is paid, in plain terms

Calling costs you nothing, and the line never sends you a bill. Pool businesses receiving the calls can pay the line something for each referral. That arrangement is the reason the number can be free, and it is disclosed at the bottom of every page on this site.

The referral fee is between the line and the company. Your price is between you and the company. Ask for the quote in writing, ask what it includes, and compare it with the published ranges in the Tucson pool service cost guide if you want a sense of the market. If a price does not sit right with you, you can turn it down and keep looking.

What you will not see on this site, and why

You will not find star ratings, customer counts, a founding date, staff photos or a list of credentials here. The line does not do pool jobs, so it has nothing truthful to put in those spots, and it will not make them up. For the same reason, it never describes any pool business as licensed, insured, certified or highly rated. It has not checked, so it stays quiet on all of that.

What the site offers instead is a way to check for yourself. Arizona's Registrar of Contractors (ROC) keeps contractor licenses in a public database that anyone can search. The ROC does not require contractors to carry liability insurance, workers' compensation aside, so ask the company directly for proof of coverage if that matters to you.

Get a company's ROC number before repair or equipment work and type it into the ROC license lookup. The record shows whether the license is active, its classification, such as R-6 for residential pool service and repair, and recent complaints. Checking is up to you, because the line does not do it.
