If you are comparing pool service companies in Tucson, it helps to know what each kind of job involves before you start calling around. This page lays out every type of pool work the free line can connect you with, in plain language, and links to a full page for each one.

Tucson Pool Cleaning Pro does not perform any of this work. It is a phone line. Call it and an automated assistant collects a quick description of the problem, then passes you to a local, independently owned pool company that serves your area. That company decides whether it can take the job, inspects the pool, quotes and bills you directly. The pages below explain what a careful company should do, so you know what to expect and what to ask.

What regular care usually covers

Pool service is the arrangement itself: how often a company comes, whether the plan price includes chemicals or adds them on, and what happens if the water turns between visits. Pool cleaning and weekly maintenance is the hands-on routine: skimming, brushing walls and steps, emptying the skimmer and pump baskets, vacuuming, reading the filter gauge, then testing the water and correcting its balance. Pool tile cleaning is a separate job for the hard white scale that builds at the waterline as water evaporates and leaves calcium behind. Weekly brushing slows that crust down, but once it hardens, removal usually takes specialized equipment.

Testing is the heart of every visit

In desert sun and heat, chlorine is used up fast. A good visit includes a water test, not just a quick look. For home pools, the CDC recommends free chlorine of at least 1 part per million (at least 2 with stabilizer in the water), with pH held from 7.0 up to 7.8. Ask the company how often it tests, what it tests for, and whether it leaves you a record of the readings.

Chemicals are a job for the professional. The CDC warns against ever combining two pool chemicals. Keep any you store in their original containers, dry, shaded and locked away from children and pets.

Repairs

When something on the equipment pad or in the plumbing fails, the first step is a diagnosis. A careful pool technician tests before replacing anything, because a pump that will not start may have a tripped breaker, a failed capacitor or a worn motor, and each means a very different bill.

Repairs and the contractor license

Arizona's Registrar of Contractors issues an R-6 license, Residential Swimming Pool Service and Repair, for servicing and minor repairs on home pools. The ROC says that classification does not include gas lines, electrical work beyond the first disconnect, plumbing connections to drinking water, or full replacement of plaster or pebble interiors. Bigger jobs go to pool contractor licenses such as B-5. So when a repair involves a gas heater line or new wiring, ask who will do that part and what license they hold.

Pool lights and pump motors involve electricity next to water. Leave the wiring to a qualified professional. Resetting a tripped GFCI or breaker once is a safe check; if it trips again, leave it off and call.

Problem water

A pool that has turned green or murky needs more than a normal weekly visit. The cleanup usually means clearing debris, running the filter hard, cleaning or replacing the filter media and bringing the chemistry back slowly over several days. It is also a safety issue: when you cannot see the bottom, nobody can see a child or pet in trouble, so keep everyone out until the water is clear. Pima County also asks residents to report neglected green pools, which can breed mosquitoes, to its vector control line at 520-724-7908.

What the line does not connect you with

To keep things clear, these jobs fall outside what the line handles. Call a company that specializes in them instead.

Building a new pool

New pool construction is not something the line connects callers with. Pool builders in Arizona work under the ROC's pool construction licenses.

Pool fencing

Barrier and fence installation is a separate trade. Pima County and the City of Tucson set barrier rules for home pools, so a fence company should know the local code.

Landscaping and pest control

Trimming the palo verde that drops leaves in the pool, or dealing with bees at the water's edge, belongs to a landscaper or pest control company.

Public and city pools

The line has no information on hours, prices or lessons at public pools. Check with the city, town or county that runs the pool.
