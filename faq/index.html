Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the most common questions about pool cleaning, repair, and maintenance in Tucson.

Weekly Service & Pricing

What is included in weekly pool service?

Every visit includes skimming the surface, brushing walls and the tile line, vacuuming the floor, emptying skimmer and pump baskets, testing and adjusting water chemistry (chlorine, pH, alkalinity, cyanuric acid, calcium hardness), checking filter pressure, and inspecting visible equipment. All routine chemicals are included in the monthly price.

How often should a Tucson pool be cleaned?

Weekly is the standard for Tucson. Desert dust, UV that burns through chlorine, and hard water that shifts chemistry all happen faster here than in cooler climates. Biweekly can work for a lightly used pool outside of monsoon season, but weekly is more reliable and catches problems before they compound.

Are chemicals included or extra?

Included. Chlorine tablets, liquid chlorine, muriatic acid, stabilizer, and any other routine chemicals your pool needs are part of the monthly price. You see one bill every month. The only exception is a large one-time treatment like an algae cleanout or a full CYA drain-and-refill, which we quote separately before doing.

How much does weekly pool service cost in Tucson?

Monthly pricing depends on pool size, whether you have a spa, and whether you run chlorine or salt. Call with your address and pool size and we will give you a flat monthly price over the phone. Chemicals are included. There are no per-visit charges and no seasonal surcharges.

Do you service salt water pools?

Yes. Salt pools still need weekly brushing, chemistry testing, and equipment checks. We inspect the salt cell every visit for calcium scale and clean it when output drops. In Pima County, salt water must drain to the sanitary sewer, not to landscape or a wash.

Tucson Water & Climate

Why is Tucson pool water so hard?

Tucson Water delivers water at roughly 220 parts per million calcium hardness, sourced from wells and the Colorado River through the Central Arizona Project. Pool manufacturers recommend staying below 200 ppm. As water evaporates in the desert heat, the calcium left behind concentrates, pushing the number even higher over time. That calcium deposits on tile, heater cores, salt cells, and pump internals.

What happens during monsoon season?

Monsoon storms dump dust, leaves, and debris into the pool and can turn water green within 48 hours. During monsoon season — mid-June through September — we increase chemical dosing and spend more time on debris removal. If a storm hits between visits and you wake up to a green pool, call and we will come out for an additional cleanup at no extra charge on most service plans.

How do I know if my pool is leaking or just evaporating?

Set a bucket of water on a pool step and mark both the pool level and the bucket level. Wait 24 hours. If the pool dropped more than the bucket, you have a leak — evaporation affects both equally. Tucson pools can lose a quarter inch per day to evaporation in summer, so a small drop is normal.

Equipment Repair

Should I repair my pool pump or replace it?

If the motor is the only problem and the pump body is in good shape, a motor swap is usually half the cost of a full replacement and gets you several more years. If the motor repair costs more than half of a new variable-speed pump, replacing makes more sense — the energy savings on a variable-speed unit pay back the difference within a year or two in Tucson's long pool season.

How much does a pool pump replacement cost?

A motor-only swap on a single-speed pump runs $250 to $500 installed. A full single-speed pump replacement is $450 to $800. A variable-speed pump replacement is $1,200 to $1,800 installed, but it cuts energy costs by 60 to 80 percent — most Tucson pool owners recover the difference within two seasons.

Why does my pool equipment fail faster in Tucson?

Three reasons: the water is harder than manufacturers spec for (220 ppm calcium), the ambient heat pushes motor temperatures higher, and pools here run more hours per year than in seasonal climates. A pump motor rated for eight years may last five to seven in Tucson. Catching a noisy bearing early is the difference between a $300 repair and a $1,500 replacement.

Do I need a permit for pool equipment repair in Tucson?

Replacing a pump, filter, heater, or salt cell with a like-for-like unit generally does not require a permit in the City of Tucson. Work that changes plumbing layout, electrical circuits, or barrier fencing usually does. If you are upgrading from gas to electric heat or adding new equipment, check with Tucson Development Services first.

Tile Cleaning

How often should pool tile be cleaned in Tucson?

Every two to three years for most Tucson pools. Water above 200 ppm calcium hardness builds a visible scale line faster than pools in softer-water cities. If you see a white or gray crust forming at the waterline, it is time regardless of the calendar.

Does tile cleaning require draining the pool?

Not for the blasting itself. We lower the water six to eight inches below the tile line, blast, then refill. A partial drain-and-refill afterward is sometimes recommended to bring calcium hardness down, which slows the next round of buildup.

What is the difference between glass bead blasting and media blasting?

Glass bead is abrasive enough to strip the glaze off ceramic and porcelain tile, leaving a rough surface that attracts scale faster. We use a calcium-ite or kieserite media that dissolves on impact — it removes the scale without scratching the tile or damaging grout. The tile comes out looking like it did when the pool was built.
