How to Keep Algae Out of Your Pool This Summer (Tucson Homeowner's Guide)

If you've ever pulled back your pool cover or walked outside on a hot Tucson morning to find your water looking green, cloudy, or slimy — you know the frustration. Algae growth is one of the most common pool problems homeowners face, and in Southern Arizona's intense summer heat, it can take over a pool in as little as 24 to 48 hours. The good news? Keeping algae out of your pool doesn't have to be complicated. With the right routine and a little know-how, you can enjoy clean, clear water all summer long.

This guide breaks down exactly what causes algae, what you can do to prevent it, and when it might be time to call in a professional pool cleaning service to handle the heavy lifting.

Why Algae Loves Tucson Summers

Algae thrives in warm, sunny conditions — which is basically a description of every day in Tucson from May through September. Monsoon season adds another layer of challenge, introducing dust, debris, and organic material into your pool that algae feeds on. Pool water temperatures regularly climb above 85°F in the summer months, creating the perfect breeding ground for green, yellow (mustard), and even black algae.

Add in high UV exposure that burns through your chlorine faster than normal, and you've got a recipe for an algae problem that can spiral quickly. Understanding this local environment is the first step to fighting back effectively.

1. Maintain Proper Chlorine Levels — Consistently

Chlorine is your pool's first and most important line of defense against algae. In summer, it's not enough to add chlorine once a week and hope for the best. The Tucson sun rapidly degrades free chlorine, sometimes cutting its effectiveness in half within just a few hours of direct sunlight.

Here's what to aim for:

  • Free chlorine: 2.0 – 4.0 ppm (parts per million)
  • Combined chlorine (chloramines): Below 0.5 ppm
  • pH level: 7.4 – 7.6 (chlorine is much less effective outside this range)

Consider using stabilized chlorine tablets (trichlor) in a floating dispenser or automatic feeder to maintain consistent levels. You should also add chlorine in the evening rather than midday so it isn't immediately burned off by the sun.

Pro tip: Use a stabilizer (cyanuric acid) to protect your chlorine from UV degradation. Keep it between 30–50 ppm for outdoor pools in Tucson.

2. Brush and Vacuum Your Pool Weekly

Algae doesn't just float in the water — it clings to your pool's walls, steps, corners, and behind ladders. These are spots where water circulation is poor and chemicals don't reach as effectively. If you only skim the surface and skip the brushing, you're letting algae set up camp in the perfect hiding spots.

Make it a habit to:

  • Brush all walls, steps, and corners with a pool brush at least once a week
  • Vacuum the pool floor to remove dead algae, dust, and debris (especially after monsoon storms)
  • Pay extra attention to shaded areas and spots behind pool equipment

After a monsoon, do a quick brush and vacuum session even if it's not your regular cleaning day. Tucson dust storms (haboobs) dump an enormous amount of organic material into pools that algae absolutely loves.

3. Run Your Pool Pump Long Enough Every Day

Stagnant water is algae's best friend. Your pool pump circulates the water and distributes chemicals evenly, which is critical for algae prevention. Many homeowners run their pumps too little in summer to save on electricity — but this false economy often leads to expensive algae treatments later.

During Tucson summers, run your pool pump 8 to 12 hours per day. If you have a variable-speed pump, set it to run at a lower speed for longer periods — this uses less energy while keeping water moving continuously.

It's also smart to run the pump during off-peak electricity hours (evenings or early mornings) to reduce your electric bill while still keeping circulation going around the clock.

4. Shock Your Pool Regularly

Even with good maintenance habits, your pool needs a deep chemical treatment — called "shocking" — on a regular basis in summer. Shocking raises the chlorine level dramatically for a short period, which kills algae spores, bacteria, and other organic contaminants before they can take hold.

In Tucson's summer heat, you should shock your pool:

  • Every 1–2 weeks as a preventive measure
  • After heavy rain or monsoon activity
  • After a pool party or any time you have heavy swimmer load
  • Immediately if you see any signs of early algae growth

Use a calcium hypochlorite shock product and follow the package directions. Always shock in the evening and keep the pump running overnight so the chemicals can fully circulate.

5. Use an Algaecide as a Preventive Measure

Algaecide is a chemical treatment specifically designed to kill and prevent algae. Many homeowners only reach for it after algae has already bloomed, but it's far more effective — and affordable — when used as a weekly preventive treatment throughout the summer.

A quality polyquat algaecide (look for 60% strength) won't foam up your pool or interact poorly with other chemicals. Add it weekly to create an extra barrier against algae growth, especially during the peak of Tucson's monsoon and heat season in July and August.

Keep in mind: algaecide is a supplement, not a replacement for proper chlorine levels and filtration. Use it alongside your regular chemical routine for the best results.

When to Call a Professional Pool Cleaning Service

Even the most diligent homeowners can run into persistent algae problems, especially during Tucson's brutal summers. If you're dealing with recurring green water, black algae spots that won't scrub off, or you simply don't have time to keep up with weekly maintenance — it's time to bring in the pros.

A professional pool cleaning service will:

  • Test and balance all your water chemistry properly
  • Brush, vacuum, and skim on a consistent schedule
  • Identify equipment issues (like poor filtration) that contribute to algae growth
  • Perform algae treatments and shock treatments as needed
  • Help you stay ahead of problems before they become expensive repairs

For many Tucson homeowners, the peace of mind and time savings of professional monthly service is absolutely worth it — especially when you consider how quickly algae can ruin a pool and how much a full algae remediation treatment can cost.
