# Research: Arizona contractor licensing, pool barriers, Recovery Fund (Tucson rebuild)

Researched 2026-10-20. Method: WebSearch only (curl and WebFetch blocked). Every item below is "verified via search summary" of the official page named; nothing was fetched directly. 11 searches used.

## 1. Arizona Registrar of Contractors (ROC) licensing

1. R-6 (Residential Swimming Pool Service and Repair) lets the licensee service and make minor repairs to residential swimming pools and their accessories. It does NOT include plumbing connections to a potable water system, gas lines, gas chlorine systems, or electrical work beyond the first disconnect, and it does not allow complete replacement of plaster or pebble pool interiors or decks.
   Source: ROC, License Classifications, https://roc.az.gov/license-classifications (verified via search summary)
2. C-6 is the commercial pool service and repair license (repair and replace commercial pool accessories and existing connections and equipment; plumbing connections, gas lines and some electrical work go to subcontractors). CR-6 is the dual license that combines the C-6 and R-6 scopes.
   Source: ROC, License Classifications, https://roc.az.gov/license-classifications (verified via search summary)
3. B-5 (General Swimming Pool Contractor) covers constructing and repairing pools and spas, including code-required barriers and utility runs from the service point to the pool equipment; other structures are excluded. It includes the B-5R subclassifications. B-6 is the same scope as B-5 plus installation and repair of solar heating devices. Dual licenses: KA-5 (commercial A-9 plus residential B-5) and KA-6 (commercial A-19 plus residential B-6, including solar).
   Source: ROC, License Classifications, https://roc.az.gov/license-classifications (verified via search summary)
4. The ROC exam requirements sheet lists R-6 with one required trade exam (with an optional T test). Exact exam wording was not readable in the summary; do not use exam details on the site.
   Source: ROC, License Classification Requirements (PDF), https://roc.az.gov/sites/default/files/2023-11/License%20Classification%20Requirements_6.8.20_0.pdf (verified via search summary, partial)
5. Minor work (handyman) exemption, A.R.S. 32-1121: the current azleg.gov text, as summarized, exempts work where the total price of the finished project, including labor and materials, is less than $1,000, the work is of a casual or minor nature, and NO local building permit is required. If a permit is required, the exemption does not apply regardless of price. Bills to raise the limit to $2,000 or $2,500 (SB1715 in 2023, HB2092 in 2024, HB2727 in 2023) appeared in results, but no result showed any of them enacted. Treat $1,000 as current only with the caveat below.
   Source: A.R.S. 32-1121, Persons not required to be licensed, https://www.azleg.gov/ars/32/01121.htm (verified via search summary); bill summaries https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/56leg/1R/summary/S.1715COM_ASPASSEDCOMMITTEE.DOCX.htm and https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/56leg/2R/summary/H.HB2092_011224_RA.DOCX.htm (verified via search summary)
6. ROC Contractor Search: https://roc.az.gov/contractor-search. It shows license status (active, inactive, suspended, revoked, expired, and so on; only a current, active license can legally contract), license classification, complaint information (complaints from the prior two years, with outcomes such as suspension or revocation), and bond details (bond type: surety, cash or certificate of deposit; bond number; bond company; amounts paid out; remaining amount; bond status). The ROC advises checking status before work starts because it can change.
   Sources: ROC, Contractor Search, https://roc.az.gov/contractor-search; ROC Contractor Search Consumer Guide (PDF), https://roc.az.gov/sites/default/files/2022-07/20201221-Guide.pdf; ROC FAQ, https://roc.az.gov/faq (verified via search summary)
7. The Contractor Search does not show insurance. Apart from workers' compensation, the ROC does not require licensees to carry insurance. (Useful for the site: tell readers to ask a company for proof of liability insurance directly.)
   Source: ROC FAQ, https://roc.az.gov/faq; ROC Consumer Guide PDF above (verified via search summary)
8. Residential contractors post a cash deposit or surety bond of $4,250 to $100,000 depending on the license (figure from an older ROC page; amounts may have changed, use only with "depending on license" wording or leave out).
   Source: ROC FAQ / Before You Hire, https://roc.az.gov/before-hire (verified via search summary, possibly dated)
9. ROC contact: 1700 W. Washington St., Suite 105, Phoenix, AZ 85007; phone (602) 542-1525; toll-free 1-877-692-9762; customer service Monday to Friday, 8:30 am to 4:30 pm. No Tucson office was found. Note: the site rules ban the word "Phoenix" in visible copy, so cite the toll-free number and the website, not the street address.
   Source: ROC, Contact Us, https://roc.az.gov/contact (verified via search summary)

## 2. Pool barriers

10. A.R.S. 36-1681 applies to a swimming pool or other contained body of water 18 inches or more deep at any point, wider than 8 feet at any point, and intended for swimming. It must be protected by an enclosure surrounding the pool area. Barrier at least 5 feet high measured on the outside. Gates self-closing and self-latching, latch at least 54 inches above the ground (or on the pool side with a low release), gates open outward away from the pool; padlock, key, electric opener or integral combination lock also allowed. Limits on climbable features on the outside, and the barrier at least 20 inches from the water's edge. Violation is a petty offense; no fine if the owner installs a compliant barrier within 45 days of citation and completes an approved pool safety course.
    Source: A.R.S. 36-1681, Pool enclosures; requirements; exceptions; enforcement, https://www.azleg.gov/ars/36/01681.htm (verified via search summary)
11. 36-1681 subsection D exceptions include: public and semipublic pools, pools or barriers built before the article's effective date, a residence where all residents are at least six years old, and political subdivisions (cities, towns, counties) that enacted their own pool barrier ordinance before the effective date, or adopt one after it that is equal to or more stringent than the statute. So in Tucson and Pima County the local code generally governs.
    Source: A.R.S. 36-1681, https://www.azleg.gov/ars/36/01681.htm (verified via search summary)
12. Pima County (unincorporated areas) residential pool and spa barriers: top of barrier at least 48 inches above grade measured on the outside; bottom no more than 2 inches above grade on the outside (Pima County flood-control policy notes 4 inches allowed over hardscape such as concrete); a 4-inch sphere must not pass through any opening; chain-link and lattice openings limited to 1.75 inches; pedestrian gates open outward away from the pool, self-closing and self-latching. Page cites chapter 3 of the 2018 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code (ISPSC). County amendment materials indicate 2024 codes, with a pool definition changed from 24 inches to 18 inches of depth; whether that is now in force could not be confirmed, so do not state the edition.
    Sources: Pima County, Barriers for Residential Pools / Spas, https://www.pima.gov/1022/Barriers-for-Residential-Pools-Spas; Pima County Regional Flood Control Technical Policy-005, https://rfcd.pima.gov/rules/pdfs/tech005.pdf; Pima County draft 2024 ISPSC amendments, https://www.tucsonaz.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/1/pdsd/documents/boards-committees-commissions/tpcjcc/070924/pima-county-240703-2024-ispsc-draft.pdf (verified via search summary)
13. City of Tucson: its 2024 building code amendments (redline draft, late 2024) apply the International Swimming Pool and Spa Code to private residential pools and change the pool depth threshold from 24 to 18 inches. Tucson pool permits include a "Pool/Spa Final/Barrier" inspection checking barrier height, self-closing and self-latching gates, and door alarms. The City's water safety page says code-required fences and self-latching gates should not be removed or altered and doors and windows to the pool area should be kept locked.
    Sources: City of Tucson Amendments to the 2024 IBC (redline), https://www.tucsonaz.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/1/pdsd/documents/boards-committees-commissions/tpcjcc/111224/cot_2024-ibc-amendments_redline-2024.10.1.pdf; City of Tucson Water Safety, https://www.tucsonaz.gov/Departments/Parks-and-Recreation/Pools-and-Splash-Pads/Water-Safety (verified via search summary)

## 3. Recovery Fund

14. The ROC Residential Contractors' Recovery Fund can compensate homeowners hurt by a licensed residential contractor's violation: up to $30,000 per claimant per residence, limited to actual damages; $200,000 maximum total per contractor license. To be eligible you must first file an ROC complaint that results in discipline of the license, or obtain a court order for payment from the Fund. (Only covers licensed residential contractors.)
    Sources: ROC, Recovery Fund, https://roc.az.gov/recovery-fund; A.R.S. 32-1132.01, https://www.azleg.gov/ars/32/01132-01.htm (verified via search summary)
