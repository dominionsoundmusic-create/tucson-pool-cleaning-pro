## (a) DOE dedicated-purpose pool pump motor rule

- Final rule: "Energy Conservation Program: Energy Conservation Standards for Dedicated Purpose Pool Pump Motors", Federal Register, published September 28, 2023, effective November 27, 2023. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/09/28/2023-20343/energy-conservation-program-energy-conservation-standards-for-dedicated-purpose-pool-pump-motors . Verified via search summary.
- Compliance dates: standard-size motors, 1.15 to 5 total horsepower (THP), must comply for motors manufactured on or after September 29, 2025. Small-size motors, 0.5 THP to under 1.15 THP, on or after September 28, 2027. Verified via search summary.
- Variable speed: the rule includes a variable-speed control design requirement. Standard-size motors (1.15 to 5 THP) must have variable speed control consistent with UL 1004-10:2022; small-size motors face a related, more stringent requirement. Source: DOE, "Dedicated Purpose Pool Pump Motors Enforcement Policy", https://www.energy.gov/gc/articles/dedicated-purpose-pool-pump-motors-enforcement-policy . Verified via search summary.
- 2026 enforcement policy: DOE enforcement policy dated April 24, 2026 says DOE will delay enforcement for distribution in commerce of small-size motors manufactured on or after September 28, 2027 but before March 26, 2029. Same energy.gov URL. Verified via search summary only (full policy PDF not seen). No change was found to the standard-size (1.15 to 5 THP) September 29, 2025 requirement.
- Background page: DOE, "Dedicated-Purpose Pool Pumps", https://www.energy.gov/cmei/buildings/dedicated-purpose-pool-pumps .
- Safe site wording: "Under a U.S. Department of Energy rule, most replacement pool pump motors from 1.15 to 5 total horsepower made on or after September 29, 2025 must have variable-speed control." Note that the rule applies to motors as manufactured, not to pumps already installed.

## (b) NEC Article 680 basics (pool lights)

- CPSC fact sheet "What is a GFCI?" says the National Electrical Code has required GFCI protection for underwater pool lighting since 1968. https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/099_0.pdf . Verified via search summary.
- CPSC "Don't Swim With Shocks" (CPSC and American Red Cross) recommends GFCIs on underwater lighting circuits operating above 15 volts, warns that older pools are the biggest concern because underwater lights may have degraded and may not be GFCI protected, and says to test permanently installed GFCIs at least monthly. https://cpsc.gov/content/dont-swim-with-shocks-cpsc-american-red-cross-warn-of-electrocutions-in-swimming-pools-hot . Verified via search summary.
- Low-voltage lighting: NFPA NEC Code-Making Panel 17 documents (2022 cycle) describe low-voltage pool equipment supplied by listed transformers or power supplies under 680.23(A)(2), with a low-voltage contact limit of 15 volts RMS for sinusoidal AC and 30 volts for continuous DC. https://docinfofiles.nfpa.org/files/AboutTheCodes/70/70_A2022_NEC_P17_SD_SRStatements.pdf . Committee-stage text, verified via search summary. Safe wording: "Low-voltage pool lights, run through a listed transformer, are also available."
- NFPA, "Protect Yourself from Electric Shock Drowning", https://www.nfpa.org/en/education-and-research/electrical/electric-shock-drowning (supporting safety source).
- Site rule reminder: no electrical how-to. Tell readers pool light work is electrical work near water and belongs to a qualified electrician or pool professional.
