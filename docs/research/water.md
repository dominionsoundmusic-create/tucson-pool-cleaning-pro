# Water research: Tucson-area providers, hardness and pools

Researched 2026-10-20. Method: WebSearch only (curl and WebFetch were blocked). No page was fetched directly; every item below is "verified via search summary" of the official URL listed unless marked otherwise. 20 searches used.

General conversion used by both Tucson Water and Oro Valley Water Utility: grains per gallon (gpg) = mg/L (ppm) divided by 17.1. Hardness is the sum of calcium and magnesium, expressed as calcium carbonate.

## 1. Tucson Water (City of Tucson)

Source water:
- Tucson Water's CAP (Colorado River) allocation is recharged into the aquifer in Avra Valley, where it blends with local groundwater, then is recovered by wells and delivered. The 2020 Main System report describes the Clearwater Renewable Resource Facility: river water goes to shallow basins, percolates down and blends with the groundwater below; the blend contains a rising share of recharged Colorado River water and varies with which Clearwater production wells are pumped. The City holds rights to about 144,000 acre-feet a year of CAP water; deliveries of the blend began in 2001.
  - Source: Water Quality & Operations Division Main System PWS AZ0410112 2020 Annual, https://beta.tucsonaz.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/1/city-services/tucson-water/water-quality/documents/2020mainsystem.pdf (verified via search summary)
- The 2025 Main System report lists three recharge and recovery projects that receive CAP water: CAVSARP (since 2001), SAVSARP (since 2008) and PMRRP (jointly owned with the Central Arizona Water Conservation District). It estimates about 78% of annual deliveries are recovered recharged water, with the rest from the Central Well Field, kept mainly for peak demand and backup.
  - Source: AZ0410112 Water Quality Report 2025 Annual Main System, https://www.tucsonaz.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/1/city-services/tucson-water/water-quality/report-archive/ccr_mainsystem_2025.pdf (verified via search summary)
  - Caution: no source found that explicitly maps the "Clearwater" name onto CAVSARP/SAVSARP. Safe wording: "Tucson Water recharges Colorado River water delivered by the Central Arizona Project into the aquifer in Avra Valley, where it blends with groundwater before it is pumped and delivered."
- Annual water quality reports (CCRs) are published each June; there is a main-system report plus separate reports for isolated well systems.
  - Source: 2025 Annual Water Quality Reports, https://www.tucsonaz.gov/Departments/Water/Water-Quality/Water-Quality-Monitoring/Report-Archive/WQ25 (verified via search summary)

Hardness:
- Tucson Water's Hard Water page: local groundwater is naturally hard; increased hardness comes primarily from mixing local groundwater with CAP water; as the percentage of CAP water increases, hardness increases. Hard water is safe to drink and not a health risk. The page advises using a pool test kit to measure the water hardness range and maintaining equipment per manufacturer guidelines.
  - Source: Hard Water, City of Tucson, https://www.tucsonaz.gov/Departments/Water/Water-Quality/Aesthetics/Hard-Water (verified via search summary)
- Tucson Water's hardness scale (mg/L as CaCO3): 0 to 60 soft, 60 to 120 moderate, 120 to 180 hard, over 180 very hard. gpg = mg/L divided by 17.1.
  - Source: Parameter Limit Explanation/Clarification table, https://www.tucsonaz.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/1/city-services/tucson-water/water-quality/parameter-explanation-table.pdf (verified via search summary; the summary noted the PDF text extraction was jumbled)
- Address-level hardness (including grains per gallon for softener settings) is available from Tucson Water's interactive water quality map: enter an address or intersection and see results from a nearby sample point.
  - Source: Easy Access to Water Quality Info, https://www.tucsonaz.gov/Departments/Water/Water-Quality/Water-Quality-Monitoring/Report-Archive/2022-Annual-Water-Quality-Reports/Easy-Access-to-Water-Quality-Info (verified via search summary)
- NO official numeric average hardness for Tucson Water was found (see "Could not verify").

Pools and conservation:
- The City's 2024 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code amendments (Exhibit G) include: "All new and remodeled exterior installed swimming pools, spas and hot tubs shall be required to be provided with a cover to limit evaporation, prior to final inspection approval." Adoption status not confirmed.
  - Source: 2024 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code Amendments Exhibit G, https://www.tucsonaz.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/1/pdsd/documents/building-code/2024-international-swimming-pool-and-spa-code-amendments.pdf (verified via search summary)
- Tucson Water conservation report: irrigation, pools and toilet issues were the leading causes of high water use found in water checkups (together 75% of reported causes; the summary did not tie this figure to a specific fiscal year with certainty). Customers can request a free Zanjero water efficiency checkup.
  - Sources: Tucson Water Conservation Program FY23-24 report, https://www.tucsonaz.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/1/water/documents/conservation/tucson-water-conservation-program-fy23-24-report.pdf ; FY2025 report, https://www.tucsonaz.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/1/water/documents/conservation/fy25-water-conservation-report.pdf ; Zanjero Program, https://www.tucsonaz.gov/Departments/Water/Conservation/Zanjero-Program-Free-Water-Efficiency-Audit (verified via search summary)
- No Tucson Water rebate specifically for pool covers was found.

## 2. Oro Valley Water Utility (Town of Oro Valley)

Sources:
- 2023 annual report: 18 groundwater production wells produced 53% of total production; four CAP wheeling locations delivered 27%; reclaimed water (1,880 acre-feet, for irrigation and other non-potable uses) about 20%.
  - Source: Water Utility Annual Report 2024 (covering 2023 data), https://www.orovalleyaz.gov/files/assets/public/v/1/documents/water-utility/2024-annual-report_final.mmv.mr.da.wuc.paa.mr.mmv.pdf (verified via search summary)
- Drinking water only: about 65% groundwater and 35% CAP water (2023 town article). The 2023 CCR says 34% of water delivered in 2023 was CAP water blended with groundwater.
  - Sources: https://www.orovalleyaz.gov/files/assets/public/v/1/documents/water-utility/2023-09-27-article-for-i-love-ov-community-sustainability-for-ov-website.paa.pdf ; OVWU Consumer Confidence Report 2023, https://www.orovalleyaz.gov/files/assets/public/v/1/documents/water-utility/consumer-confidence-reports/ccr-10-164-2023-ov-final-pdf.pdf (verified via search summary)
  - Safe wording: "Oro Valley's drinking water is mostly groundwater, blended with Central Arizona Project water (about a third of 2023 deliveries)."

Hardness:
- Water quality page: hardness in the Oro Valley Water Utility service area ranges from 36 ppm to 270 ppm. Hardness is not a health risk and is not regulated by state or federal agencies. Scale: soft 0 to 60 ppm, moderately hard 61 to 120, hard 121 to 180, very hard over 180. For a specific neighborhood, call 520-229-5000.
  - Source: Water Quality, https://www.orovalleyaz.gov/Government/Departments/Water-Utility/Water-Quality (verified via search summary)
- 2024 annual report (2023 data): 2.1 to 15.8 grains per gallon (36 to 270 ppm); the majority of water tests moderately hard to hard; hardness varies with the volume of CAP water being blended in a given pressure zone.
  - Source: Water Utility Annual Report 2024, URL above (verified via search summary)
- Earlier years: 2022 was 38 to 320 ppm (2023 annual report); 2021 was 36 to 250 ppm; 2020 was 37 to 250 ppm.
  - Source: 2023 annual report, https://www.orovalleyaz.gov/files/assets/public/v/1/documents/water-utility/water-utility-commission-reports/2023-annual-report.pdf (verified via search summary)

## 3. Metro Water District (Metropolitan Domestic Water Improvement District)

Service areas:
- Metro Main (largest, northwest metropolitan Tucson): about 23 square miles per the 2024 CCR (another District source says about 26), roughly between Lambert Lane and River Road, Thornydale Road on the west and First Avenue/Oracle Road on the east; 17,953 active customers (figure from District information page per summary). Supplied by 25 active wells (2024 report).
- Metro Hub: northeast Tucson.
- Metro Southwest: Diablo Village, E&T and Lazy B systems (Lazy B also receives water through a connection with Tucson Water).
- Metro West: west of the Marana Airport, annexed 2005, no customers currently served.
- Marana and Oro Valley run their own utilities (Marana Water, Oro Valley Water); Metro Water partners with both through the Northwest Recharge, Recovery and Delivery System.
  - Sources: Service Area Maps, https://metrowater.com/about-us/metro-waters-service-areas/ ; District Information, https://metrowater.com/about-us/district-information/ ; 2024 Water Quality Report Metro Main, https://metrowater.com/wp-content/uploads/2024-CCR-Report-A-Metro-Main.pdf ; Contact Us, https://metrowater.com/contact-us/ (verified via search summary)

Hardness:
- Hardness comes from naturally occurring minerals in groundwater. The District's hardness page has tables for Metro Main, Metro Hub and Metro Southwest; systems are interconnected in some areas so water is blended and concentrations may vary.
  - Source: Hardness / Total Dissolved Solids / Fluoride, https://metrowater.com/water-resources-quality/hardness-total-dissolved-solids/ (verified via search summary; current numbers not returned)
- 2017 Metro Main hardness map (OLD data): individual systems ranged from 56 ppm (3.2 gpg) to 170 ppm (9.9 gpg), with values including 81, 96 and 150 ppm.
  - Source: 2017 Metro-Main Hardness Concentrations, https://www.metrowater.com/wp-content/uploads/2017_Metro-Main_Hardness_Map.pdf (verified via search summary). If used, label it as 2017 data.

## 5. Vail

- Vail Water Company: its CAP allocation is delivered to Tucson Water's recharge facilities, stored, recovered and sent through Tucson's distribution system to Vail's service area ("wheeling"), via the Old Vail Reservoir and booster station. Tucson Water says wheeling to Vail Water Company has run since 2013; Vail Water's own site says it has received CAP water for customers since May 4, 2015. Vail Water's ACC annual report (year ended 12/31/25) describes its supply as 100% CAP water received directly from the City of Tucson, already chlorinated. CAP allocation in its surcharge tariff: 1,857 acre-feet.
  - Sources: Old Vail Reservoir, Tucson Water, https://www.tucsonaz.gov/Departments/Water/Dollars-at-Work/Old-Vail-Reservoir ; Vail Water, https://vailwater.com/ ; ACC annual report, https://www.azcc.gov/docs/default-source/utilities-files/water/annual-reports/vail-water-company/vail-water-company.pdf?sfvrsn=da4d32e7_3 ; 2025 CAP surcharge approval letter, https://vailwater.com/uploads/2025-CAP-Surcharge-approval-letter.pdf (verified via search summary)
  - Vail Water Company is a separate private company, not part of Tucson Water.
- Rincon Water Company (near Vail): assets acquired by Global Water Resources in January 2022; about 79 active connections over roughly 9 square miles at year end 2023.
  - Source: Global Water Resources Form 10-K FY2023, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1434728/000143472824000087/gwrs-20231231.htm (verified via search summary)
- Some Vail-area homes may also be in Tucson Water's service area (Tucson Water's Old Vail Reservoir serves its southeastern customers), but exact boundaries were not verified.
- Hardness for Vail: not found.

## 7. Green Valley ("who provides water for Green Valley AZ")

- Community Water Company of Green Valley: the main provider; groundwater system (federal PWS dataset lists about 22,085 people served); took over operations June 10, 1977; has pursued Central Arizona Project water to ease pressure on the aquifer. Office 1501 S. La Canada Drive, Green Valley 85622, 520-625-8409.
  - Sources: About Community Water Co. of GV (Green Valley Sahuarita Chamber), https://mms.greenvalleysahuarita.com/gvscc/mp_CommunityWaterC ; Sahuarita Resource Directory, https://sahuaritaaz.gov/resourcedirectory ; Project Renews Community Water Company, https://scenicsantaritas.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Project-Renews-Community-Water-Company.pdf (verified via search summary; not utility-owned pages)
- Green Valley Domestic Water Improvement District (GVDWID): public district formed by residents in May 2003; service area south of Mission Twin Buttes, mostly on the west side of I-19; about 4,412 residential and over 70 commercial connections.
  - Source: Green Valley Water District (chamber listing), https://mms.greenvalleysahuarita.com/gvscc/mp_GVDWID (verified via search summary)
- Farmers Water Co (Continental system, PWS AZ0410049): groundwater, about 4,290 people, 1,716 connections, listed in Green Valley, Pima County.
  - Source: reference.geoconnex.us PWS record, https://reference.geoconnex.us/collections/pws/items/AZ0410049?f=html (verified via search summary)
- Valle Verde del Norte Water Cooperative: over 100 member households in the Valle Verde del Norte community.
- Safe answer: "Green Valley has more than one water provider. The largest is Community Water Company of Green Valley; others include the Green Valley Domestic Water Improvement District and Farmers Water Co. Supplies are local groundwater. Your water bill shows which one serves your home."
- Hardness: not found.

## 8. Recommended pool calcium hardness range

- CDC Model Aquatic Health Code (2023, 4th edition) inspection cross-reference guide lists calcium hardness at or below 1,000 ppm for public pools/spas (MAHC sections 5.7.4.4.3 and 5.7.5.6). No minimum found in the summary.
  - Source: Cross-Reference Guide for the Aquatic Facility Inspection Form, https://www.cdc.gov/model-aquatic-health-code/media/pdfs/Aquatic-Facility-Inspection-Cross-Reference-508.pdf (verified via search summary)
- State public-pool rules: Maryland COMAR 10.17.01.45 requires calcium hardness between 150 and 400 ppm; Georgia Rule 511-3-5-.17 lists a 150 ppm minimum and 1,000 ppm maximum.
  - Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/maryland/COMAR-10-17-01-45 ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/georgia/Ga-Comp-R-Regs-R-511-3-5-.17 (verified via search summary)
- The commonly cited 200 to 400 ppm residential range appeared only in non-official sources in this search. Safe wording: "Public pool codes generally set a floor around 150 ppm (Maryland requires 150 to 400 ppm), and the CDC Model Aquatic Health Code caps public pools at 1,000 ppm. Ask your pool professional or plaster manufacturer what target fits your surface." Note these are public-pool rules, not Arizona residential rules.
